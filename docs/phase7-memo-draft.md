# Product decision memo — owner approved

**Prioritize a test restoring basic playback controls for free users.** The issue is users being forced to shuffle, unable to go back, seek, repeat, play in order or pick a song, and encountering skip limits. One review puts it plainly: “to play in order buy premium” ([review 7e271329](phase7-cohort-spotcheck-v3.md)). The cohort is named **Free-tier playback controls locked behind Premium**; some restrictions are inferred rather than explicitly attributed to Premium.

**Why this issue.** Other—unspecified ranks first under the contract’s severity-sum rule, with 36,225 reviews ([B1](phase7/claims.csv), [S10](phase7/secondary-claims.csv)). It represents general dissatisfaction with no stated cause, so it is not a specific fix target. Controls rank second in both the propagated baseline and random-prefix-only view ([S1](phase7/secondary-claims.csv), [S2](phase7/secondary-claims.csv)).

Controls barely change between views: **4,444 reviews / 13,877 severity points** in the baseline ([B2](phase7/claims.csv), [B3](phase7/claims.csv)), versus **4,440 / 13,865** in the random prefix ([S3](phase7/secondary-claims.csv), [S4](phase7/secondary-claims.csv)). Ads fall from **6,037 / 12,965** ([B4](phase7/claims.csv), [B5](phase7/claims.csv)) to **4,088 / 9,071** ([S5](phase7/secondary-claims.csv), [S6](phase7/secondary-claims.csv)). Exact-text propagation gives repeated short complaints such as “Too many ads” additional weight by copying their labels to matching rows outside the prefix. In the seeded random sample, controls carry approximately **1.5×** the severity-weighted complaints of ads ([S7](phase7/secondary-claims.csv)). Baseline mean severity is approximately **3.1 for controls versus 2.1 for ads** ([B6](phase7/claims.csv), [B7](phase7/claims.csv)).

Absolute counts cover only classified rows. The random prefix is a uniform sample of all nonempty reviews, so its proportions are the best estimate of the full mix; the baseline adds 1,949 additional ad rows ([S11](phase7/secondary-claims.csv)) but only 4 controls rows ([S12](phase7/secondary-claims.csv)). Remaining uncertainty comes from sampling error and lexical grouping, not the direction of this comparison.

**Proposed next step.** Test restoring back, seek and repeat for a subset of free users. Measure complaint rates, one-star rating rates and Premium conversion against a comparison group, using predefined observation windows and decision criteria. These controls appear to be used as upgrade incentives, so the test should weigh any change in Premium conversion against the reduction in complaints. An alternative is an ad-frequency cap. These are proposed experiments, not claims of causal impact, revenue gains or reduced churn.

**Decision limits.** Completed labels cover approximately **36% of nonempty rows** ([S8](phase7/secondary-claims.csv)); approximately **65% of classified complaint/cancellation rows have no specific cause assigned** ([S9](phase7/secondary-claims.csv)). Grouping uses final, spot-checked lexical rules (v3), which can misassign or miss reviews. Verifier agreement is not human accuracy; the verifier and fallback share the same model.

*Evidence key: B references are checker-compatible baseline claims; S references are separately documented secondary analyses, not baseline claims. Engineering provenance and unavailable-usage disclosures are in the README. Owner-approved revision of the saved model draft.*
